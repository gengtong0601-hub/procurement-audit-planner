Lesson 3 of 4
The human gate
Record the auditor's decisions and have the register revised to match.
Nothing goes further until an auditor approves the risks. The risk-assessor has already written
outputs/auditor-comments.md
, a blank decision row for every risk. You fill it in, and the risk-assessor applies your decisions.
In this guide you record decisions by telling Claude Code what they are. The file remains the record.
By the end of this lesson
outputs/auditor-comments.md
holding your decisions, and a revised register that records what changed.
Steps
Step 4.1
Open the decision template
The risk-assessor wrote
outputs/auditor-comments.md
alongside the register in step 3.7: one row per risk with its score and scope, and blank Decision and Comment cells.
A blank Decision means undecided, so the template alone changes nothing. Each time the register is rebuilt the template is rewritten to match it, and any decisions already recorded are kept.

Step 4.3
Revise the register
Approved risks stay as they are. Amendments are applied and flagged. Rejections move to the excluded list with your reason.
The revision is checked, not just written.
build_register.py
reads your decisions and refuses to build if one was left unapplied, if a risk changed that you did not ask about, or if an approved risk has gone. The revision header and the closing decisions section come from the builder, never from the model's hand.
Prompt
Paste into Claude Code
Copy
Run the risk-assessor again. Apply the decisions in outputs/auditor-comments.md and mark the register as a revision.
What to expect
The register header says it is revision 1, dated from your comments, and lists R-09 (amended) and R-10 (rejected).
R-09 keeps the score you set, flagged as an auditor override, and its justification still carries a real citation.
R-10 sits in the excluded list with your reason, and the closing section lists all three decisions and how each was applied.

What you have built
A system that turns a council's rulebook into testable rules, analyses the full spend population against them, folds in past audit history, and produces a rated risk register that an auditor approves.
Every quote is verified against its PDF. Every figure comes from a named script. Every rating cites a rule, a metric or an audit item.
Next, an agent team of a planner, a challenger and a QA reviewer turns the approved risks into a planning pack, argues over it, checks it, and brings it back for your sign-off.
